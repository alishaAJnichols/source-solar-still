Why this exists

Clean water shouldn't depend on a supply chain

Source is a tool for designing your own solar water still — sized to your actual household, climate, and space. No subscriptions, no proprietary parts.

The bigger idea: knowledge like this should circulate locally, not stay locked behind a single seller. Build one, learn from it, and what you figure out helps the next person too.

Before you build, watch your actual site for a full day if you can. In the Northern Hemisphere, south-facing spots get the most consistent sun. Avoid anywhere shaded by trees, roof overhangs, or buildings during 10am–2pm — that's when solar intensity peaks and matters most.

Shade patterns shift with the seasons. A spot that's fully sunny in summer can fall into shadow in winter as the sun's angle drops lower in the sky — check both if you're building something permanent.

Ground needs to be flat and stable — if the basin sits crooked, water won't pool correctly toward the collection edge.

Raise it slightly above grade so rain runoff doesn't pool underneath and undermine the base. Keep it away from anything flammable, and close enough to where you'll store the water that you're not hauling it far.

Watch wind exposure — too much wind cools the glass and can reduce condensation. A partial windbreak (fence, wall) helps without blocking the sun. If you keep livestock, fence it off or place it out of reach so animals don't damage the glass.

Two tools you'll lean on most: a caulk gun (for sealing every joint) and a power drill (for the frame). Both are safe and quick to learn with a little guided practice — a few solid video walkthroughs:

  • How to use a caulk gun — beginner's guide
  • Caulking gun tips, including the built-in cutter/poker
  • Power drill basics for beginners

General safety: always wear eye protection, keep hands clear of the bit, drill a pilot hole before driving screws into wood, and practice your caulk line on cardboard before doing the real seams.

Silicone sealant: 5–10 years before it dries, cracks, or loses grip, depending on UV and heat exposure. Check it yearly.

Polycarbonate glazing: ~10 years before the UV coating degrades and it starts yellowing; tempered glass lasts much longer but is more fragile.

PEX or silicone tubing: 5+ years — inspect yearly for cracking or stiffness.

HDPE basin liner: 5–10 years; metal basins can last decades but may need repainting to keep the black heat-absorbing surface.

Rigid foam insulation: effectively indefinite unless physically damaged or waterlogged.
